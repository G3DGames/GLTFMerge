Description

Swaps body parts and outfit pieces between glTF models that share a skeleton.

Modular character kits, such as the Quaternius outfits, come as skinned meshes rigged to the same skeleton as a base character: a pair of boots, trousers, a hood. This unit puts such pieces on a loaded model, hides the body under them and takes off what they replace.

TglTFPartLibrary loads part files and lists every skinned mesh in them as a TglTFPart, with the bones that drive it: for each triangle, the bone with the most weight on its three corners. The bones that drive a good share of a part's triangles are the ones it covers; see TglTFPartOptions.

TglTFPartApplier puts parts on a model:

  • Each part is copied in with its mesh, materials, textures and images, and bound to the model's skeleton by bone name, so it follows the model's bones.

  • A part put on earlier that the new part covers for the most part, such as the old boots, is taken off: deleted with whatever only it used. This goes by bones, as new sandals do not lie over the old boots.

  • The body triangles a part's surface lies over are hidden: left out of the index list. This goes by shape, not by bone, as a bone is often only partly covered: trousers that end at mid-calf leave the shin below them. Both the body and the parts are skinned to the model's skeleton at rest, and a body vertex is covered when a part's surface crosses the line along its normal within a small gap, on either side, so body that pokes through the cloth is hidden too. The body keeps all its vertices, and the hidden triangles come back when the part covering them is taken off.

Parts added are marked in their node's extras (see GLTF_PART_EXTRA), so they are recognised after the model is saved and loaded again. The model's other skinned meshes, such as hair under a hood, are hidden where covered like the body; they are never deleted.

The applier remembers each body primitive's triangles from before the first change, so taking parts off and putting others on never loses any. That memory lasts as long as the applier: after the model is saved and loaded again, its triangles as saved are the starting point. 

part files --> TglTFPartLibrary --> TglTFPart (mesh + bones it covers)
TglTFPartApplier.Apply(parts):
  for each part:  take off the parts it replaces --> copy it in, bound by bone name
  then:           hide the body where the parts lie over it --> delete unused data

Progress is reported through OnProgress, so loading and applying can run in a background thread. Use a library or an applier from one thread at a time; only RequestAbort and AbortRequested may be used from another.

function ReadPrimitiveTriangles(ARoot: TglTF; APrimitive: TglTFMeshPrimitive): TArray<Cardinal>;

Reads the triangles of a mesh primitive.

Triangle strips and fans are turned into separate triangles. A primitive without indices uses its vertices in order.

Parameters
ARoot
The tree the primitive is in.
APrimitive
The primitive.
Returns

Three vertex indices per triangle; nil for points and lines.

Exceptions raised
EglTFError
The data is missing or does not fit.
function ReadAccessorValues(ARoot: TglTF; AIndex: Integer): TArray<Double>;

Reads the values of an accessor as numbers.

Normalized integers are turned into 0..1 or -1..1, as the glTF specification says; sparse values are applied. Matrices are read as their components in order, without the padding some small matrix types have.

Parameters
ARoot
The tree the accessor is in.
AIndex
Index in ARoot.Accessors.
Returns

Count times the number of components values; element I's component C is at I * components + C.

Exceptions raised
EglTFError
The data is missing or does not fit.

Constants

GLTF_PART_EXTRA = 'glTFViewerPart';

Name of the extras entry that marks a node as a part put on by TglTFPartApplier.

Its value is an object with "file", the part file's name, and "part", the part's name.
